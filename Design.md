# Zazu Design

## 1. What Zazu looks like

Zazu is a soft, warm, editorial-style app. It feels like a gentle morning ritual rather than a productivity tool. The visual language is built around a blush-to-lavender gradient backdrop, translucent glass cards, rounded controls, and a calm, premium finish.

The app is not sharp, dark, or aggressively modern. It is more like a quiet, beautiful object you open at the start of the day.

## 2. The visual system, exactly as implemented

### Core background
The app uses a three-stop gradient background that moves from a warm peach tone to a cool blue tone and then to a soft lavender tone.

The colors are:
- warm peach: #fde8d8
- cool blue: #dde8f8
- soft lavender: #ede0f8

This gradient is applied across the shell of the app and creates the sense of a layered, atmospheric environment.

### Night mode
In dark mode, the gradient shifts to a deeper, moodier palette:
- deep plum: #1a1225
- midnight blue: #1a1830
- near-black: #0e0c1a

The app does not go stark black. It stays rich, soft, and slightly velvety.

### Glass cards
Most content surfaces sit on translucent glass cards with a soft blur and a thin border.

The visual behavior is:
- slightly translucent white fill
- soft border outline
- rounded corners
- subtle blur behind content
- content appears layered over the gradient without feeling heavy

These cards are the primary surface for word content, premium upsells, success states, onboarding content, and settings sections.

### Typography
The app uses two type families:
- a serif display face for headings, hero words, and emotionally significant moments
- a rounded sans-serif face for body copy, labels, and utility UI

The typography feels editorial and calm. Words are large and centered when they need attention, but the UI remains readable and approachable.

### Buttons
Buttons are rounded, pill-shaped, and simple. The primary CTAs are filled and confident, while secondary actions are lighter and outline-based.

The visual feel is:
- soft rounding
- strong contrast
- low visual noise
- clean spacing
- no heavy shadows unless needed for emphasis

### Accent color
Gold is used sparingly and intentionally. It marks premium value, special moments, and reward states.

The gold tone is:
- #c9963a

It is not used everywhere. It is used to signal worth, reward, and premium identity.

## 3. The app’s actual look by surface

### Home / Today
The home experience should feel like a calm morning dashboard.

It has:
- a full-screen gradient background
- a soft card-based content layout
- a prominent word-of-the-day hero area
- small progress and streak indicators
- rounded actions for the main next step
- a quiet sense of motion and warmth

The home screen is meant to feel like a gentle welcome into the day, not a crowded dashboard.

### Alarm flow
The alarm flow feels more focused and immersive than the rest of the app.

It uses:
- a full-screen gradient shell
- a centered word experience
- a calm but slightly more dramatic treatment than the home screen
- large typography for the word and its meaning
- simple actions for continue or dismiss
- a soft glow or warm highlight behind the core content

The alarm experience is the emotional center of the app.

### Learn screen
The learn screen is clean and editorial.

It presents:
- the word as the clear centerpiece
- pronunciation and definition beneath it
- a short explanation or etymology block
- a calm, uncluttered layout with plenty of whitespace

This screen should feel like reading a beautiful note rather than filling out a form.

### Morning task screen
The morning task screen is more interactive, but still calm.

It should feel like:
- a centered prompt
- rounded options or answer choices
- a clear primary action
- soft support for correctness or feedback
- visual rewards that feel encouraging rather than flashy

### Success screen
The success screen is warm and celebratory without being loud.

It uses:
- a softer, more luminous glow than the rest of the app
- a reward-based presentation for streaks and coins
- a polished, complete feeling after the flow is finished

### Word Gym
The gym experience is more structured and game-like, but it still inherits the same visual language.

It uses:
- the same gradient shell
- glass cards for puzzle content
- clear spacing around each round
- small reward and progress cues
- a slightly more utility-focused layout than the alarm flow

### Calendar
The calendar should feel like an archive of the user’s learning history.

It should feel:
- more informational than emotional
- organized and readable
- lightly premium through cards and subtle accents
- soft rather than dense

### Settings
The settings screen is straightforward and calm.

It should feel like:
- clearly grouped sections
- rounded cards and rows
- readable labels and toggles
- no visual clutter
- a trustworthy, polished place for account and preferences

### Gold / Premium paywall
The premium screen is the most overtly “premium” part of the app.

It should feel:
- elevated and polished
- clear about the benefit of upgrading
- reassuring rather than pushy
- visually richer than the free experience
- supported by a premium card or feature list with gold accents

The premium surface should not feel like a generic store screen. It should feel like a quality upgrade path for the app’s core ritual.

## 4. The glow and depth language

Zazu uses light, soft glow effects rather than harsh neon lighting.

### Glow behavior
The glow is subtle and atmospheric. It is most visible in:
- the alarm flow
- the success experience
- premium/upsell moments
- the deepest parts of the gradient shell

The glow should not feel like a technical effect. It should feel like morning light, warmth, or a soft halo around the content.

### What the glow should feel like
- gentle
- warm
- soft-edged
- low-contrast
- inviting

It should support the mood of the app, not take over it.

## 5. UI component language

### GradientBackground
This is the base shell for most screens. It provides the soft atmospheric background and creates continuity between surfaces.

### GlassCard
This is the primary container for content. It creates the layered, polished look that defines the app’s visual identity.

### PrimaryButton
Primary buttons are rounded and filled, using the app’s primary ink tone and clearly readable text.

### Outline buttons
Secondary actions use a lighter border-based style and softer text contrast.

### Toggles
Toggles should feel like a clean, modern control but still match the soft visual world of the app.

### Badges and chips
Small status indicators, part-of-speech badges, streak chips, and coin moments should use soft background shapes and minimal contrast, keeping the UI refined.

## 6. Interaction feel

The app feels calm because its interaction design is restrained.

The visual rules are:
- no aggressive motion
- no loud transitions
- no cluttered state changes
- clear feedback without visual noise
- reward moments should feel satisfying, not flashy

## 7. What the app should never feel like

Zazu should not feel like:
- a fintech app
- a gaming app
- a harsh productivity tool
- a dark crypto-style UI
- a generic subscription funnel

It should feel like a quiet, beautiful learning ritual that happens gently at the start of the day.

## 8. Design summary

The app looks like this:
- soft peach-blue-lavender gradients
- translucent glass cards
- rounded, calm interface controls
- serif headlines for emotional emphasis
- sans-serif body text for clarity
- warm gold accents for premium moments
- subtle glows that feel like morning light
- a premium, editorial, low-pressure experience

That is the visual identity of Zazu. Every screen should reinforce that identity.
